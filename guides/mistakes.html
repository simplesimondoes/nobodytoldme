The human part

What to say when you make a mistake.

Golf has a way of making small errors feel public. The good news is that recovery is part of the game too.

By Sye Anthony · A little social insurance for the course

You will hit a poor shot. You will forget whose turn it is. You will stand in the wrong place. At some point you will probably make a noise during someone else’s swing and spend the next five minutes wishing you could reverse time.

Every golfer has done this. The difference between an awkward moment and an awkward afternoon is usually what happens next.

Keep the apology simple

“Sorry, that was my mistake” is enough. You do not need a speech. A long explanation can make the other person feel responsible for reassuring you, which is not a job they applied for.

Fix what can be fixed

If you have moved a ball, replace it where it was. If you have damaged the green, repair it. If you have slowed the group, be ready for your next shot. A practical correction is more convincing than a dramatic promise never to do it again.

Do not make the whole round about it

One mistake should not become the story of every hole. Notice it, learn from it, and return your attention to the people you are playing with. Golf is difficult enough without carrying an old moment around in your pocket.

Give other people the same room

The kindness you want after a poor shot is usually the kindness someone else wants too. Let a bad shot be a bad shot. Offer help when it is welcome. Save the technical analysis unless somebody asks.

Good etiquette is not perfection. It is the habit of making the next moment easier for the people around you.

The invisible part of the game

Nobody Told Me Either is about the standards that make golf more welcoming, not more intimidating.
