When it counts

Golf competition etiquette.

The score matters more in a competition. It still does not matter more than how you treat the people sharing the round.

By Sye Anthony · Nerves, scorecards, and keeping perspective

Your first competition can feel like someone has quietly changed the rules of being a golfer. The same course is there. The same clubs are in your bag. Suddenly every pencil mark seems to have legal consequences.

Know the format

Before you tee off, make sure you understand the format, handicap allowance, playing order, and how the scorecard is being kept. Ask early. A question on the first tee is much easier than a mystery on the eighteenth.

Respect the scorecard

Write clearly, check the hole number, and compare scores at the end of each hole when appropriate. In a competition, the scorecard is not a diary of your emotional journey. It is a record. Treat it carefully.

Play your own game

Competition etiquette includes not commenting on another player’s club choice, line, or score unless the format requires it. Encourage people, but do not coach them without invitation. Sometimes the kindest thing you can say is nothing at all.

Keep the pace

Nerves can make every shot feel like it deserves a committee meeting. Take your preparation seriously, then play. The people behind you are competing too, and they deserve a clear course.

Win well and lose well

Shake hands. Thank your playing partners. If you win, be pleased without turning the result into a personality. If you lose, let the result be information rather than a verdict. There will be another competition, and there will almost certainly be another three-putt.
